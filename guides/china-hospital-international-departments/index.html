China's hospital international departments: what foreigners actually pay

Viral videos show $10 hospital visits in China. Foreigners usually use international departments, which run separately and charge more. Here's how they work.

The viral "$10 hospital visit" videos usually show regular public clinics. Foreigners who want English service, appointments and privacy usually go to a hospital's international department, which runs separately and charges more. Even so, prices are often far below US cash prices: one Shanghai international department lists a basic checkup at US$377.

The viral story, and what it leaves out

A UK content creator's account of getting a diagnosis and tests in Beijing, including a sedated endoscopy, within five days for about 2,800 yuan (US$400) spread widely online. The story is real. But it took place at a public hospital, not an international department, and her UK wait for a specialist was the main reason she went.

Videos showing $10 registration fees are also real. They're local public-clinic prices, and they leave out the rest: tests, interpreters, and the separate services most foreign patients use.

What international departments are

  • Separate units inside large hospitals, with their own appointments, staff and prices
  • English-speaking staff in many of them, and private rooms
  • Higher fees than routine public care
  • Small: under Chinese rules, international services are limited to under 10% of a hospital's services

What they cost

There's no single published price list. Examples:

Premium packages with more imaging cost more. Ask for an itemized quote, in writing.

What a basic package can include

The Shanghai basic package lists: a physical exam; blood, urine and stool tests; liver, kidney, glucose and lipid panels; thyroid function; an H. pylori breath test; two tumor markers (AFP, CEA); an ECG; a low-dose chest CT; abdominal and thyroid ultrasound; and sex-specific exams.

Note the tumor markers and chest CT: US guidelines don't recommend either for most people at average risk. See our over-screening guide.

How big is this trend?

China's National Health Commission reports 1.28 million international patient visits to major hospitals in 2025, up 73.6% from 2022, with visits from Europe and North America doubling. These are government figures; they count visits, not people, and include foreigners living in China.

Five questions before booking

  1. Is the price itemized, and does it include the English report?
  2. Is the final report in English, signed by a doctor? When will I get it?
  3. Will I get my scan files (DICOM)?
  4. Who explains the results to me, in English, before I leave?
  5. What happens if something is found?

Getting there

Americans need a visa for an ordinary round trip to China. A trip through China to a third country may qualify for 240-hour visa-free transit. See our entry rules guide.
