Entry rules for Americans planning care abroad (2026–27)

Thailand cut visa-free stays to 30 days, Korea's K-ETA exemption ends 31 December 2026, and China still needs a visa unless you're transiting. Here's the current picture.

Several rules changed in 2026. Thailand cut visa-free stays for Americans from 60 to 30 days on 15 September 2026. Korea's K-ETA exemption for US citizens ends on 31 December 2026. China still requires a visa for a simple round trip, though a trip through China to a third country can use 240-hour visa-free transit. Always check the embassy site the week you book.

Thailand: 30 days, tourism only

Thailand's 2024 scheme allowed Americans 60 days visa-free. A new scheme published in August 2026 took effect on 15 September 2026: up to 30 days, for tourism only. A one-time extension of up to 30 days may be granted at an immigration office. A checkup trip is usually well within 30 days, but longer recovery stays may need a visa.

South Korea: K-ETA is coming back

Korea temporarily exempted US citizens from its electronic travel authorization (K-ETA). That exemption runs to 31 December 2026. From 1 January 2027, Americans need an approved K-ETA before boarding. Apply only through the official K-ETA website, and allow time for approval.

China: visa, or transit to a third country

China doesn't offer Americans visa-free entry for an ordinary round trip, so most US travelers need a tourist visa. The exception is 240-hour visa-free transit: if you arrive from one country and leave for a different one, you can stay up to 10 days in permitted regions, entering and leaving through designated ports.

That makes a combined trip possible, for example US → Seoul → Shanghai → US: a checkup in Seoul, then up to 10 days in China without a visa. Confirm the route, ports and regions with China's National Immigration Administration and your airline before booking.

Other things to check

  • Passport validity: many countries want at least six months left on your passport.
  • Arrival cards: some countries now use digital arrival forms (Thailand's TDAC, for example).
  • Travel advisories: check the US State Department's level for your destination and any regional warnings.
  • Insurance: see our guide on travel medical and evacuation cover.
