After care abroad: records, follow-up and hidden costs

What to bring your doctor after a checkup abroad (report, DICOM scan files, lab ranges), and why cheap surgery abroad often isn't cheap once follow-up is included.

After a checkup abroad, bring your doctor a signed English report, your scan files in DICOM format, your lab results with normal ranges, any pathology reports, and the hospital's contact details. Call ahead to ask how your doctor's office accepts outside images. For surgery, the quote usually covers only the operation; follow-up care, insurance gaps, extra time and travel after surgery are the costs people leave out.

Part 1: bringing your records home

What to bring

  • Written report in English, signed by the hospital doctor
  • Scan files (DICOM) on a disc, USB drive or download link, not just printed images or photos
  • List of tests done, with the lab's normal ranges
  • Pathology reports, if a biopsy was taken or a polyp removed
  • The hospital's contact details and the examining doctor's name
  • Receipts, if you'll claim anything from insurance

Before your appointment

  • Call your doctor's office and ask how they accept outside images (upload portal, disc, USB).
  • Ask whether they need the report translated by a certified translator.
  • Expect that your doctor may repeat some tests, especially imaging. That's their decision, based on what they need.

What we don't do

We don't interpret results. That's for the hospital doctor who examined you, and your own doctor.

Part 2: why cheap surgery isn't always cheap

We start with checkups, not surgery, for a reason. With surgery, the price quoted abroad is rarely the full cost.

Hidden costWhy it matters
Follow-up at homeSome US doctors are reluctant to take over complications from surgery done abroad
Insurance gapsMany travel policies exclude complications from treatment that was the reason for the trip
Extra timeLonger stays, changed flights, more days off work
Flying after surgeryAsk the surgeon when it's safe to fly; long flights after surgery carry risks
Revision or repeat proceduresIf something needs redoing, who pays, and where?

If you're considering surgery abroad anyway

  • Get a written plan for complications before you pay anything: who treats them, where, and who pays.
  • Line up a doctor at home who will see you afterwards.
  • Look at medical travel complication insurance, which is sold separately.
  • Allow enough recovery time before flying home.
