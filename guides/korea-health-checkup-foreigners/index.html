Health checkups in Seoul for foreigners

Seoul's university hospitals run checkup centers for foreign patients with English reports. Here are price ranges, what drives the cost, and the questions to ask.

Seoul's large university hospitals run dedicated checkup centers that serve foreign patients, with English reports. A July 2026 guide puts their packages at roughly US$610 to US$15,500, depending on the hospital and how much imaging and endoscopy is included. The main draw is getting many tests done in one day, at one place.

Why Korea

  • One-day format: checkup centers are built to move patients through many tests in a day.
  • Large hospitals: the best-known centers belong to major university hospitals.
  • English reports: the major centers provide them, though format, timing and fees vary.
  • Safety and entry: the US travel advisory for South Korea is Level 1, and no visa is needed (a K-ETA is required from 1 January 2027).

What drives the price

  • Low end: blood and urine tests, physical exam, ECG, chest X-ray, abdominal ultrasound.
  • Middle: adds gastroscopy and/or colonoscopy, often with sedation.
  • High end: adds CT, MRI or PET-CT, specialist consultations and one-to-one interpreters.

More isn't automatically better. See our guide on over-screening before choosing a premium package.

How it compares with the US

A US clinic's self-pay core checkup costs about $3,400–3,600, and a screening colonoscopy adds about $3,000. Korean mid-range packages that include endoscopy can cost less than that, but the top Korean packages cost as much or more. The clearest savings are for people paying cash, on packages that include endoscopy or imaging.

Questions to ask before booking

  1. Is the English report included, or extra? How long does it take?
  2. Will I get my scan files (DICOM), not just printed images?
  3. Who explains the results to me, and in what language, before I leave?
  4. What happens if something needs follow-up while I'm in Korea?
  5. Which tests are included, item by item, and which are optional?
  6. How is sedation handled for endoscopy, and do I need someone with me afterwards?

Use registered agencies only

Korea requires agencies that bring in foreign patients to register with the government, and caps the fees registered agencies can charge hospitals. If you use an agency, ask for its registration.
