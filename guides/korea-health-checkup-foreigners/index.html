Health checkups in Seoul for foreigners

Seoul's university hospitals run checkup centers for foreign patients with English reports. Price ranges, how far ahead to book, how to fit a checkup into a Seoul trip, and the questions to ask.

Seoul's large university hospitals run dedicated checkup centers that serve foreign patients, with English reports. A July 2026 comparison puts their packages at roughly US$610 to US$15,500, depending on the hospital and how much imaging and endoscopy is included. A full package takes a morning or most of a day. The big centers ask you to book one to eight months ahead, so if you already have a trip to Seoul planned, ask early.

Why Korea

  • One-day format: checkup centers are built to move patients through many tests in a day.
  • Large hospitals: the best-known centers belong to major university hospitals.
  • English reports: the major centers provide them, though format, timing and fees vary.
  • Safety and entry: the US travel advisory for South Korea is Level 1 (exercise normal precautions), and Americans need no visa for visits of up to 90 days. For entry from 1 January 2027, see below.

What drives the price

  • Low end: blood and urine tests, physical exam, ECG, chest X-ray, abdominal ultrasound.
  • Middle: adds gastroscopy and/or colonoscopy, often with sedation.
  • High end: adds CT, MRI or PET-CT, specialist consultations and one-to-one interpreters.

More isn't automatically better. See our guide on over-screening before choosing a premium package.

Fitting a checkup into a Seoul trip

  • Book early. In the same July 2026 comparison, the big centers ask for bookings well ahead: at least a month for Samsung, at least three months for the Seoul National University Gangnam Center, about four months for Severance, and seven to eight months for Asan. An imaging center such as Hanshin Medipia takes bookings a few days ahead.
  • Set aside a morning, or most of a day. The comparison lists about 5 hours for Samsung's Executive Women package and about 6 hours for Asan's Intensive package. A whole-body MRI at Hanshin Medipia takes about an hour.
  • Put it early in the trip. Packages with blood tests or endoscopy usually mean fasting beforehand; follow the center's own instructions. An early slot leaves days for the results talk, or for a follow-up test, before you fly home.
  • After sedation, keep the day free. If your package includes sedated endoscopy, ask whether you need someone with you, and plan nothing else for the rest of that day.
  • Trips from 1 January 2027: plan on a K-ETA. Americans are exempt from Korea's online travel authorization (K-ETA) until 31 December 2026. Korea has extended the exemption a year at a time, most recently in December 2025, but no further extension has been announced. Unless one is, you'll need a K-ETA before you fly. Apply only on the official site, k-eta.go.kr.

How it compares with the US

A major US clinic's self-pay core checkup (2024 price list) costs about $3,400–3,600, and a screening colonoscopy adds about $3,000. Korean mid-range packages that include endoscopy can cost less than that, but the top Korean packages cost as much or more. The clearest savings are for people paying cash, on packages that include endoscopy or imaging.

Questions to ask before booking

  1. Is the English report included, or extra? How long does it take?
  2. Will I get my scan files (DICOM), not just printed images?
  3. Who explains the results to me, and in what language, before I leave?
  4. What happens if something needs follow-up while I'm in Korea?
  5. Which tests are included, item by item, and which are optional?
  6. How is sedation handled for endoscopy, and do I need someone with me afterwards?

Our guide to what happens if a checkup abroad finds something covers question 4 in more detail.

Use registered agencies only

Korean law requires anyone who brings foreign patients to a hospital to register with the Ministry of Health and Welfare, and unregistered brokering is a criminal offense. The ministry also caps the commission a registered agency can take from a hospital: 15% of the medical bill at the largest hospitals, 20% at general hospitals and 30% at clinics. If you use an agency, ask for its registration.
