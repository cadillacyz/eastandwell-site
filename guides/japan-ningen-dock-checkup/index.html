Japan's ningen dock: the full-day checkup

Japan's one-day ningen dock checkup is thorough and well established. Here's what a standard day includes, typical yen prices, and how to handle the language gap.

A ningen dock is a one-day, self-paid checkup that's been part of Japanese life for decades. A standard day at an English-capable Tokyo clinic is listed at about ¥73,000 including tax, and about ¥145,000 with a head MRI and MRA. Those are prices for people living in Japan. Visitors without Japanese insurance often pay more: one national hospital charges them double. Japan's strengths are high standards and safety. Its main hurdle for Americans is language.

What the name means

"Ningen dock" means "human dry dock": like a ship, you come in for a full inspection.

What a standard day includes

Contents vary by center. A typical one-day course includes:

  • Physical exam and medical history
  • Blood and urine tests (lipids, glucose, liver and kidney function, thyroid, blood counts)
  • ECG
  • Lung function test
  • Chest X-ray
  • Abdominal ultrasound
  • Stool test for hidden blood
  • Eye and hearing tests
  • Bone density (often)
  • Stomach check by barium X-ray, or gastroscopy for an extra fee
  • Sex-specific exams

Some centers add tumor markers as standard. As with any package, ask which items fit your age and history (see our over-screening guide).

Expect anything from a morning to most of a day, depending on the course, with fasting from the night before.

What it costs

ExampleListed price (including tax)
Tokyo Midtown Clinic, standard checkup¥72,600
Same clinic, with head MRI/MRA¥145,200
Same clinic, executive courses¥330,000 and up

These are prices for people living in Japan. Tokyo Midtown Clinic quotes visitors from abroad separately. The national hospital in Tokyo that was the National Center for Global Health and Medicine charges people without Japanese health insurance twice its listed price, and asks them to book through an approved agent that provides an interpreter. Ask any center for its price for visitors, in writing, before you book. For US dollars, convert at the day’s exchange rate.

The language gap

English support varies a lot. Some hospitals have international patient teams that speak English, and sometimes Chinese. Others ask visitors to book through an approved agent who provides the interpreter. Before booking, ask:

  1. Is an interpreter included, or extra?
  2. Will the final report be in English? When?
  3. Will I get my scan files (DICOM)?
  4. Who explains results to me before I leave?

Getting there

Americans can visit for up to 90 days without a visa, and the US travel advisory for Japan is Level 1. Japan is also the closest of the Asian checkup destinations to the US West Coast.
