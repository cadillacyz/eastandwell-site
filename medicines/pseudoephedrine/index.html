Can I bring Sudafed (pseudoephedrine) to Japan, South Korea or China?

Checked 14 October 2026 · Independent guide by East&Well.

Short answer

  • Japan: it depends on the product: over 10% pseudoephedrine needs a permit.
  • South Korea: up to 6 bottles or a 3-month supply.
  • China: not on China’s lists, and we haven’t found a rule for travellers.

Country by country

Japan

Check before you pack

The rule

Japan treats pseudoephedrine as a “stimulants’ raw material”. Products with 10% pseudoephedrine or less are excluded; above that you need permission before you travel. US labels don’t show the share.

What to do

Ask your doctor or pharmacist for one without pseudoephedrine. Or ask Japan’s Narcotics Control Department about your exact product at least 14 days before you arrive.

Sources

Japan’s Narcotics Control Department: controlled substances list (PDF, 12 Dec 2024); Japan’s Narcotics Control Department: application guidance

Checked 14 October 2026

South Korea

Within the limit

The rule

Ordinary medicines: up to 6 bottles or a 3-month supply. You must be able to show they’re for your own use: a prescription, a medical certificate or the pharmacy label.

What to do

Keep them in the original packaging. You may be asked for a receipt.

Sources

Korean government notice: bringing medicines into Korea; US Embassy in Korea: controlled substances

Checked 14 October 2026

China

Not checked yet

The rule

Pseudoephedrine isn’t on China’s 2025 lists of controlled medicines, and we haven’t found an official rule for travellers.

What to do

Ask the Chinese embassy or consulate near you before you pack it.

Sources

China’s 2025 lists of controlled medicines (NMPA announcement No. 55)
