Can I bring Ritalin, Concerta (methylphenidate) to Japan, South Korea or China?

Checked 14 October 2026 · Independent guide by East&Well.

Short answer

  • Japan: no permit needed up to 2.16 g in total.
  • South Korea: a permit from Korea’s drug regulator is needed, at least 10 working days before you arrive.
  • China: a Category I controlled medicine: up to one prescription’s worth, with a certificate of diagnosis.

Country by country

Japan

Within the limit

The rule

Methylphenidate is on Japan’s list of psychotropics. You can carry up to 2.16 g in total for your own use with no paperwork.

What to do

More than that: carry a certificate from your doctor naming the illness, why you need the medicine, and the medicines and amounts. More than a month’s supply: contact Japan’s health ministry (yakkan@mhlw.go.jp).

Sources

Japan’s Narcotics Control Department: application guidance; Japan’s Narcotics Control Department: table of psychotropics (PDF)

Checked 14 October 2026

South Korea

Paperwork before you fly

The rule

Methylphenidate is a controlled medicine in Korea (a psychotropic). You need a permit from Korea’s Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) before you arrive.

What to do

Submit the complete application at least 10 working days before you arrive (weekends and Korean public holidays don’t count): two application forms, a full passport copy, your flight booking, a letter from your doctor and your prescription. A permit covers up to 90 days; you can ask for up to 5 extra days’ doses in case of delays. Carry it in your hand luggage. Only the person who takes the medicine can get the permit.

Sources

Korea’s drug regulator (MFDS): guideline on permits for your own medicine; Korea’s Narcotics Control Act Enforcement Decree, appendices (law.go.kr); US Embassy in Korea: controlled substances

Checked 14 October 2026

China

Paperwork before you fly

The rule

China lists methylphenidate as a Category I psychotropic. You may carry up to the most a single prescription allows, with a certificate of diagnosis from a medical institution and your passport.

What to do

Get a certificate of diagnosis from your doctor before you go, and declare the medicine to customs (the red channel).

Sources

China’s 2025 lists of controlled medicines (NMPA announcement No. 55); Chongqing government: carrying medicines into China (29 Jun 2026)
